Pharmakon AtlasΔιοσκουρίδου Περὶ ὕλης ἰατρικῆς

Dioscorides recorded about 800 medicinal substances around 70 CE. The Atlas reads the Greek text, lines every plant and use up against modern research, and ranks the ancient claims that science has barely examined. Each lead comes with the passage, the preparation to replicate and the papers to start from.

The track questionWhat did centuries of texts know about medicinal plants, and does modern science agree?

This page answers the second half by measuring how much modern research exists for each ancient claim. A missing study is a research gap, not a disagreement.

1 · Read the Greek

Wellmann's edition, 822 chapters, parsed into plant, part, use category, route and preparation.

πινόμενον μετ᾿ οἴνου → taken, with wine

2 · Identify the plant

Greek names resolved to genera through the Liddell–Scott–Jones lexicon, with a confidence level.

μήκων → Papaver

3 · Count modern evidence

Europe PMC, strict title queries per genus and indication, clinical studies counted separately.

0 matching studies → gap

4 · Compare preparations

Ancient vehicles (honey, vinegar, wine) against what modern studies actually tested (ethanol, methanol).

Where the gaps are

Scored plant–use pairs by category. Ancient authors wrote most about the digestive tract and skin; the sparse evidence is concentrated in eye, urinary, gynaecological and antidote claims.

Method and limits

Every number on this page comes from code in the repository. These are the weak points.

What was measured

  • Extractor against Staub et al. 2016 (5,314 uses, 206 genera, 1,237 gold pairs): category F1 0.68, category plus route F1 0.48.
  • Plant names: 213 genera; LSJ-only mappings are medium confidence and the list marks them.
  • Europe PMC audit of retrieved papers, 3rd round: 54% relevant, 16% irrelevant.

What is not solved

  • The extractor is lexicon-based, not an LLM; some passages show a nearby sentence rather than the use itself.
  • The audit was judged by the author. A human spot check is still to be done.
  • Counts depend on query design: papers on "opium" or "morphine" are not matched to Papaver.
  • Preparation mismatch is a heuristic, and the artemisinin analogy is our hypothesis. Novelty is unverified.

How to read a gap

  • "Research gap" means no matching study in Europe PMC titles and abstracts with these queries. It does not mean the claim is wrong.
  • Safety flags are raw counts of toxicity papers. Several leads (hellebore, poppy) are toxic at doses used in antiquity.
  • Translations shown are unreviewed drafts.
